Components/M4 · Cost

M4 · Day 05 · Mid-sprint

What will it cost?

A planning forecast, then a mid-sprint re-forecast that knows what has already been spent, with the drivers behind every number.

Component
Sprint cost forecasting
Researcher
Hewaduwa H.G.V · IT22186706
ProblemResearch gapObjectivesMethodResultsIn the productLimitations
01 · Problem

Costed once, discovered at the end

Sprint cost is usually estimated once, at planning, from a spreadsheet or an expert's judgement. By the time an overrun is visible, the sprint is over.

When the number does move, nobody can say which factor moved it, so there is nothing to act on.

02 · Research gap

Forecasts that never update

  • Parametric models estimate cost up front and aren't revised as work happens [11].
  • Earned value management tracks spend against plan but extrapolates rather than learning what remains [13].
  • Forecasts are rarely explained factor by factor.
  • ML is often assumed to beat simple formulas without testing it at each stage.
03 · Objectives

What M4 set out to do

Main objective. Forecast sprint cost at planning and re-forecast it mid-sprint from the cost already incurred, explaining every forecast.

  • Compare a formula and an ML model at planning, and ship whichever wins.
  • Predict remaining effort mid-sprint and add the actual cost already incurred.
  • Explain each forecast with exact Shapley values over nine factors.
  • Show the forecast and its trend inside the sprint view.
04 · Method

A hybrid, by evidence

At planning, the sum of task estimates multiplied by the blended rate was compared with an ML candidate on a time-ordered holdout. The formula won (MAPE 23.45% vs 25.65%), so it ships.

Mid-sprint, a gradient-boosting model predicts the remaining effort from progress so far, and the forecast adds the actual cost already incurred. Drivers are computed as exact Shapley values over nine factors, all 512 coalitions.

Sprint plan

Task estimates, team members and their rates at planning time.

Formula forecast

Sum of task estimates × blended rate. Chosen because it beat the ML candidate.

Mid-sprint signals

Work completed, effort logged and actual cost incurred by day five.

Gradient boosting

Predicts the effort still to come; the actual cost so far is added to it.

Exact Shapley drivers

Nine factors, all 512 coalitions, so each driver's share is exact.

Forecast with drivers

The re-forecast, its change since planning, and what moved it.

05 · Results

A forecast that learns from what was spent

Why this matters

Halfway through a sprint, adding the cost already incurred to a learned forecast of the remaining effort is far more accurate than trusting the original remaining estimates.

Why this matters

At planning there is little signal beyond the estimates themselves. The ML model didn't beat the simple formula on the time holdout, so the formula ships. Choosing by evidence is part of the method.

Drivers

Exact Shapley values

Every forecast is explained over nine cost factors, evaluating all 512 coalitions exactly rather than sampling them [14].

Hybrid

Chosen by evidence

Planning uses the formula; mid-sprint uses the model. Each stage ships whichever method won on held-out data.

Time-ordered holdout. Training data is synthetic; validation on real project cost data is ongoing.

06 · In the product

Where it shows up in SprintIQ

The cost forecast widget sits on every sprint, with the cost trend and the drivers behind each change.

07 · Limitations

What we can't claim yet

  • Training data is synthetic; real cost data may differ in rates and overheads.
  • At planning the ML model did not beat the formula, so planning-time accuracy is the formula's.
  • MAPE of 28.63% mid-sprint is a large improvement, but still a wide margin for budgeting.
