Components/M1 · Estimation

M1 · Day 00 · Planning

How big is it, and how much can we do?

Story points and next-sprint velocity, each predicted with a range that keeps its promise and the reasons behind it.

Component
AI-based effort & sprint estimation engine
Researcher
Cooray B.D.A · IT22189530
ProblemResearch gapObjectivesMethodResultsIn the productLimitations
01 · Problem

Planning poker is a vote, not a forecast

Story points are usually set by planning poker: the team votes, discusses outliers and settles. It works, but it anchors on the first loud number, drifts with optimism, and gives no sense of how uncertain the result is. Velocity, the points a team can finish per sprint, is read off the last few sprints, which says little when the team, the domain or the scope has changed.

Teams then over-commit. The gap only shows at the sprint review, after the plan has already failed.

02 · Research gap

What existing estimators leave out

  • Most story-point models return one number with no calibrated range, so a confident 5 and a wild-guess 5 look the same.
  • Published models are often not compared with simple baselines, and a replication found they frequently don't beat the median [5].
  • Velocity models assume history from the same domain and break for a team in a new one.
  • Few estimators explain which parts of a story drove the number.
03 · Objectives

What M1 set out to do

Main objective. Build an estimation engine that predicts story points and next-sprint velocity with calibrated ranges and explanations, and that works for teams in new domains.

  • Predict story points from story text and structure, beating the median baseline.
  • Give a P20–P80 range that meets its 70% coverage target, using conformalised quantile regression.
  • Snap estimates to the Fibonacci scale teams already use.
  • Explain every estimate with its top TreeSHAP drivers.
  • Predict velocity zero-shot for an unseen domain, and improve with few-shot retraining.
  • Turn the velocity band into a commitment verdict inside sprint planning.
04 · Method

Two models, one envelope

Story points come from a gradient-boosted model over 150 TF-IDF text terms and 34 structural features. Two LightGBM quantile models give the P20 and P80 bounds, which CQR then calibrates on held-out data so that the range keeps its promised coverage. Every output is snapped to 1-2-3-5-8-13 and explained with TreeSHAP.

Velocity uses an XGBoost model trained without the domain feature, so it can predict for any domain from day one. When a team builds up history, a few-shot weighted retrain fine-tunes it, and a domain adapter routes each prediction to the fine-tuned model when one exists.

Story-point pipeline

Story text and structure

Title, description and acceptance criteria, plus structure such as dependencies, sub-tasks and labels.

150 TF-IDF terms + 34 features

Text becomes 150 weighted terms; structure becomes 34 numeric features.

XGBoost point estimate

A gradient-boosted model predicts the most likely size.

LightGBM P20 / P80

Two quantile models predict the low and high ends of the range.

CQR calibration

Conformalised quantile regression widens the bounds by δ = 0.594 so 70% of true values fall inside.

Fibonacci snap

Value and bounds snap to 1-2-3-5-8-13, the scale the team plans on.

Value, range, drivers, reason

The estimate is shown with its range, its top TreeSHAP drivers and one sentence of explanation.

Velocity pipeline

Sprint history and capacity

Completed points, team size, availability and carry-over from recent sprints.

16 features

History and capacity summarised into 16 features, deliberately without the domain.

Domain adapter

Uses the team's fine-tuned model when one exists, otherwise the universal model.

Velocity band

A predicted range of points the team can finish next sprint.

Commitment verdict

Compares what the team committed with the band: under, within or over-committed.

06 · In the product

Where it shows up in SprintIQ

The estimate panel sits on every story; the velocity panel sits on every sprint. Each suggestion has Accept, Override and Dismiss, and the choice is recorded with the prediction.

07 · Limitations

What we can't claim yet

  • Both models were trained on synthetic data. Real organisational data may behave differently, and validation on it is ongoing.
  • Exact Fibonacci matches are 41.0%; the range, not the point, is the honest output.
  • Snapping widens the range: coverage rises to 89.7%, at about two Fibonacci steps wide.
  • The few-shot fine-tune needs a handful of completed sprints before it helps.
