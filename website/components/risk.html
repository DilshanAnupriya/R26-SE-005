Components/M2 · Risk

M2 · Day 07 · In flight

What could go wrong?

Three risks scored live as the board moves, with how bad, how likely, why, and what to do next.

Component
Sprint risk prediction
Researcher
Jayasinghe J.A.N.T · IT22122100
ProblemResearch gapObjectivesMethodResultsIn the productLimitations
01 · Problem

Found out on day nine

Risk is discussed at sprint planning and then mostly forgotten until it lands: a budget overrun at the review, a requirement that changed mid-sprint, a communication breakdown nobody flagged.

By the time a risk is visible on a burndown chart, the cheap moment to act has passed.

02 · Research gap

Snapshots, not signals

  • Risk models score a snapshot taken at planning and don't react to what happens on the board [15].
  • Most report a probability without saying how severe the outcome would be.
  • Text signals in comments and descriptions are rarely used for communication risk.
  • Predictions stop at a score; they don't recommend what to do.
03 · Objectives

What M2 set out to do

Main objective. Predict sprint risks, update them live from board events, and turn each one into recommended next steps.

  • Predict over-budget and requirement-change risk with two heads each: how bad and how likely.
  • Detect communication and collaboration risk from text with a fine-tuned DistilBERT model.
  • Adjust scores live from board events such as blockers and scope changes.
  • Explain each risk and recommend steps with an LLM advisor.
04 · Method

Two heads per risk, adjusted live

For each risk, CatBoost predicts two things: how bad it would be (Low, Medium, High) and how likely it is [16]. One pair of models covers over-budget risk and one covers requirement change. A fine-tuned DistilBERT model reads text for communication and collaboration risk [17].

During the sprint, board events adjust the scores live. An LLM advisor then explains each risk in plain language and recommends steps.

Sprint metrics and board events

Planned vs completed work, blockers, scope changes and comment text.

CatBoost × 2 heads

Severity (Low / Medium / High) and likelihood for over-budget and requirement change.

DistilBERT

A fine-tuned model scores communication and collaboration risk from text.

Live adjustments

Board events move the scores during the sprint, not just at planning.

LLM advisor

Explains each risk and recommends next steps the team can take.

06 · In the product

Where it shows up in SprintIQ

The sprint risk dashboard shows the three risks and their daily trend; the advisor answers with remediation steps.

07 · Limitations

What we can't claim yet

  • Requirement-change prediction is weak (F1 0.459); it is flagged as such in the product and to the Copilot.
  • Training data is synthetic; real sprints may show different risk patterns.
  • The advisor sends sprint data to a third-party LLM, so it is off until an organisation turns it on.
